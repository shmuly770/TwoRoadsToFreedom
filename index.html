Two Roads to Freedom

The American Revolution, as David McCullough tells it in 1776, set beside the Exodus from Egypt.

The Exodus and the American Revolution are both stories of a people breaking free from a ruler who refused to let them go. In both, the lowest moment came just before the turning point, and in both the turn came at the edge of the water: the Yam Suf and the icy Delaware. Each story teaches that freedom takes emunah, courage and the willingness to take the first step. But they part at the end. Bnei Yisrael were freed by Hashem's open miracles and led to Har Sinai to accept the Torah, while the Americans won by their own effort and wrote their own laws. The Exodus shows freedom as a gift that comes with a covenant; the Revolution shows freedom as something people must build and guard themselves.

Where the comparison breaks

The Exodus

A miracle that ends at a covenant

In the Exodus, Hashem does the saving. The makkos and the splitting of the sea are open miracles, and Moshe tells the people to stand still and watch. The goal is not only to stop being slaves to Pharaoh but to become servants of Hashem, which is why the journey does not end at the sea but at Har Sinai, where Bnei Yisrael accept the Torah. Their freedom comes with a covenant and with mitzvos.

The Revolution

Human politics that end at a constitution

In 1776, people do the saving, and they make mistakes. Washington loses New York, generals argue, soldiers desert, and the army survives through planning, luck, bad weather for the enemy and stubborn courage. The Americans were not leaving their land; they were fighting to rule it themselves. Their freedom ends in a constitution that people wrote and can change, not a Torah given from Heaven.

What the difference means

The two stories look alike on the road but lead to different places. The Revolution shows what people can do when they refuse to give up. The Exodus shows that real freedom is more than escaping a king: it means choosing a higher purpose. Putting them side by side helps explain why the American founders loved the Exodus story, and also why the Torah's version asks more of us than any war can win.
